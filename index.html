Warm-up · vote first

You paste six lines from Stack Overflow into an assignment. Do you cite it?

Hands up. Every hand gets counted before anyone gets an answer.
Every one of you was right.

And that is the problem. Four defensible answers means you are each running a private policy you have never written down. Private policies fail under deadline pressure, and they fail in front of a review board.

For the next 13 minutes we replace instinct with a procedure.

The spine of the next 13 minutes

Integrity is not a virtue. It is four decisions,
made at four moments.

01

Before you start

02

The pressure point. Almost every violation lives here.

Skipping any one of these is how good people produce misconduct. Notice that only the fourth is about honesty. The first three are about planning, and planning is a skill, which means it is learnable.

Decision 1 of 4 · Code

Same logic. New names. Is this a violation?

Source A · found on Stack Overflow
def get_avg(data): # compute the sum s = sum(data) return s / len(data)
Source B · what the student submitted
def calculate_mean(input_list): # total up the values val_sum = sum(input_list) return val_sum / len(input_list)
Yes. Renaming is not authoring.

Identical logic with new variable names is paraphrase without attribution. Prose gives you quotation marks and block quotes to mark borrowed text. Code gives you exactly one tool: the comment. Adapted from a source, inspired by a source, or copied outright, say which, in one line, above the function. That line is cheaper than the meeting you will otherwise have with the Dean of Students.

Decision 1 continued · the part that follows you to work

Attribution is an academic rule. Licensing is a legal one.

In the course

Copying verbatim without a comment is plagiarism. It is graded, reported, and it stays in your student record.

In production

Copying a GPL-licensed snippet into proprietary code is a licensing violation. Nobody grades that. Counsel handles it, and your employer inherits the exposure.

The habit that satisfies both: check the license, note it in a comment, adapt rather than paste. Four seconds now, and the same four seconds protects you in a code review five years from now.

Decision 2 of 4 · Generative AI

Four things that will get you in trouble faster than plagiarism.

Disclosure is not optional

Using AI to draft prose or code without saying so is a violation, even when the output is good.

Hallucinated sources are yours

A fabricated reference in your literature review is read as fabrication. The tool does not take the hit.

Prompt overlap looks like collusion

Similar prompts produce near-identical output. Two identical submissions look coordinated whether or not you ever spoke.

The default rule

Unless your syllabus says otherwise, using AI counts as unauthorized help from another person. Silence is not permission.

Decision 2 of 4 · vote before the reveal

Read the policy. Then read what the student did.

From the syllabus

"GenAI is permitted only for brainstorming and outlining. All final code and prose must be authored by the student."

What happened

A student writes all their own code, then uses ChatGPT to generate the comments and documentation. To save time.

Violation?

Yes, it is a violation.

Comments and documentation are prose, and they ship inside the final submission. The policy says all final prose. Intent does not change what was submitted.

The habit: when a policy feels ambiguous to you, that is not a loophole. It is a prompt to send one email before the deadline. That email is also your evidence.

What it costs · Case 1

COMPAS: a risk score used in U.S. courts.

In 2016, ProPublica examined an algorithm that predicts whether a defendant will reoffend, and reported a pattern in who it got wrong.

Defendants who did not reoffend, but were labeled high risk
Black defendants0%
White defendants0%

The failure was not a bug

The model was trained on historical arrest and sentencing data. It learned the patterns in that data accurately. Those patterns were not neutral, so neither was the score.

Northpointe, the vendor, disputed ProPublica's analysis. That dispute is about which definition of fairness applies, which is precisely the judgment you will be asked to make.

What it costs · Case 2

Amazon's resume screener taught itself who belonged.

What it learned

The system downgraded resumes containing the word "women's," as in women's chess club captain. It penalized graduates of all-women's colleges. It rewarded verbs that appeared more often in resumes written by men.

Why

It was trained on ten years of resumes submitted to a male-dominated industry. It concluded, correctly given its data, that male candidates were the pattern for success.

Amazon found the problem and scrapped the tool. That is the part worth copying. Nobody there set out to build a discriminatory filter, which is exactly what makes the next decision difficult.

Decision 3 of 4 · the one with a power gradient

Your model is perfect. If you drop 5% of the data.

You are a research assistant. Excluding one demographic group's data makes your results clean. Your advisor is under pressure to publish, and controls your funding and your letters.

Vote honestly

Not what the ethics board would want. What you would actually do at 11 p.m. with an advisor waiting on the draft and your funding on the line.

If nobody in the room picks A, nobody in the room is being honest.

B, and the reason matters.

The excluded 5% is not noise. It is the finding. A model that fails on one demographic group has told you something true about itself, and dropping the evidence is data manipulation regardless of how small the subset is. This is the mechanism behind both cases you just saw.

The practical version: you are rarely choosing between honesty and survival. Write it into the limitations section, and put your concern in email. Documented concerns protect you.

Decision 4 of 4 · the moment it actually happens

Nobody plans to cheat at the start of a project.

They run out of time. Then a bad option starts looking like the only option.

Answer this one privately, right now

In your current semester, which assignment are you most likely to be finishing at 2 a.m. the night it is due?

Ask, do not assume

Asking early is a professional skill. Every senior person you admire got there by asking.

Reusing your own work is still a violation

Submitting prior work needs written approval from every instructor involved. Yes, even your own paper.

Report accurately

Internships, participation, results, and the reason you need the extension. Honesty about the timeline is part of it.

Ninety seconds, silent, nothing is recorded

Check only what is true today.

Whatever you left unchecked is your actual risk. Not a character flaw, just an unfinished task. Pick one and finish it this week.

Find it now, not in week eleven

Two minutes. Phones out. Call it out when you find it.
